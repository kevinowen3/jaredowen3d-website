HD or 4K: which looks better on YouTube?

Four versions of the same 30-second Falkirk Wheel sequence. Open each one on YouTube, set the playback quality by hand, and compare them on the same screen.

Clip 1 is the baseline: the video as it is published today. Clips 2 and 3 take that same HD footage and upscale it to 4K in ffmpeg, without and with sharpening. Clip 4 is the real thing, re-rendered at 4K in Blender.

How to compare fairly

YouTube makes it easy to think you are watching 4K when you are not. A few habits keep the comparison honest.

  1. Set the quality by hand. In the player, open the gear icon, choose Quality, and pick 2160p for clips 2 to 4 and 1080p for clip 1. The Auto setting often picks a lower stream, especially right after you seek.
  2. Give it a few seconds. After seeking, YouTube may briefly serve a lower-quality stream while it buffers. Wait, or scrub back a few seconds, before judging a frame.
  3. Pause on the same moment. Open each clip in its own tab and freeze on a matching frame. Thin edges such as railings and cables, sky and water gradients, and any on-screen text show differences most clearly.
  4. Know what your screen can show. On a 4K display you see the extra resolution. On a 1080p screen you mostly see the higher bitrate YouTube gives 4K uploads, which shows up as fewer compression artifacts and cleaner edges.
  5. Missing the 2160p option? YouTube processes the 4K version of an upload after the HD version. If 2160p is not in the menu yet, the upload is still processing. Check back later.
  6. Use the full screen. Watch each clip in fullscreen mode rather than the small embedded player, so the pixels are shown at the size a viewer would see them.
